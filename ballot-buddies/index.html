    How this works

    San Francisco publishes each ballot card as a separate record. On mail ballots, cards usually stay in scan order, so we group them back into whole ballots. Some groups will be wrong. Election Day cards are shuffled and can't be grouped, so "people like you" means mail and QR voters. SF percentages use every counted card, including Election Day. For ranked races, we use your first choice. Judicial retention races are not included.

    A statistical model sorts those ballots into about 200 small groups and four larger blocs. Blocs look only at how people voted, so a skipped race doesn't move yours. It never shows a real ballot. Your Buddies are the small groups that best match your picks, and that includes races outside your district. No real ballot has both, so the model learns each race from the voters who had it, and uses your pick as a clue to which group you fit. Those picks don't count toward your rarity score. The map shows estimates by area, and small counts use the wider bloc's pattern instead.

    On held-out ballots, the model got about 80% of a hidden card right, compared with 68% from citywide totals. Neighborhoods are assigned by each precinct's center, so edges are approximate. Endorsements come from Sway voting groups and from each group's own published slate, and may contain errors.
